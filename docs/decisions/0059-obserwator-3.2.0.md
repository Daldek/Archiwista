# ADR-059: Obserwator 3.2.0: tiers, hourly and weekly series, groundwater, in index schema 1

**Date:** 2026-11-23
**Status:** accepted (step A1 of Nogat's plans `2026-11-16-operational-series.md` and `2026-11-20-hydromet-providers.md`, approved on 2026-11-23 for the author, as Obserwator's ADR-030 records; Nogat ECO-084, ECO-085; the coordinator's rulings of 2026-11-23 on private versions, the year type of groundwater data and the charts); amends ADR-002 (an addition within the index's version 1 is made in place), ADR-021 (the units week and hour), ADR-032 decision 12 (a series of an unknown unit is skipped, not unreadable) and ADR-045 decision 9 (no chart of weeks or hours)

**Context:** Obserwator 3.2.0 (tag `v3.2.0`) adds to version 1 of its formats, under Nogat's ECO-085, what ECO-084 and its ADR-030 to ADR-032 decided: the optional sidecar fields `tier` (`telemetry`, `operational`) and `publication` (`public`, `private`) on every version of a dataset that is not the verified archive; captures of operational data as archive versions of the raw root, one a dataset and UTC day, sealed by `store sync` from a spool in `<state_dir>/obserwator/captures/`; sub-daily series of `time_unit` `hour` (`pl.imgw.hydro_subdaily`, `cz.chmu.hydro_subdaily`), one version per calendar month in a slot `series:<key>:<YYYY-MM>` of its own; and ČHMÚ's groundwater: the station kind `groundwater` with `groundwater_kind` `bore` or `spring`, a daily and a weekly series (`time_unit` `week`, each reading at its own date), the variables `H_asl` [m] and `spring_yield` [l/s], and statistics (`cz.chmu.groundwater_characteristic`, `time_unit` `statistics`) that form no series. ECO-085 keeps these at `/1` only if every consumer can skip what it does not know and is changed and deployed before the producer writes them. Archiwista 0.3.3 could not: its adapter refused a series of an unknown unit as a producer error (`series_unreadable`, on every scan), its index's `CHECK (time_unit IN ('day', 'month', 'half_year'))` rejected the row, the month slots were not series slots at all, and its index, at `SCHEMA_VERSION` 1 until the first release (REL-1.4, ECO-072), knew only a rebuild for a changed schema.

**Options and decision:**

1. **Hourly and weekly series are indexed with their coverage.** Options: A) skip them as units the index does not know (ECO-085's minimum: versions listed, no coverage); B) index their versions and read their coverage in their own units. A leaves the bores' weekly record (from 1933) and the only sub-daily record up to yesterday without labels, Timeline or card, while the label arithmetic (SPEC 4.8.4) already counts any unit that maps to whole numbers. Decision: B. `TimeUnit` of the source interface is `day`, `week`, `month`, `half_year` or `hour`; `index/series.py` counts a week as the whole weeks from Monday 1 I 1 and an hour as the day's ordinal times 24 plus its hour; `series_catalogs` takes both units.
2. **A week is a calendar week named by its Monday and belongs to the year of its Monday.** Options: A) ISO 8601 week-years (the year of the week's Thursday); B) a 7-day grid from each series' first reading; C) the year of the Monday. Obserwator counts the weeks Monday to Sunday and names a gap by the Mondays of its first and last missing week (its ADR-032 D2), so B would disagree with its gaps, and A would move a week that starts on 29 December into the next year for no reader's benefit. Decision: C. A year's last week may end in the next year, and the range R never runs past the year of the last known day. A variable's `first` and `last` are the dates of readings; a gap runs from the Monday of its first week to the Sunday of its last.
3. **An hour is a UTC hour.** Options: A) local time, as Obserwator's GUI shows it; B) UTC. The series and their coverage are UTC, and local time repeats an hour each autumn. Decision: B. A known period (days) covers every hour of its days; the API gives an hourly series' `first`, `last` and gap ends as UTC times `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SSZ` (`datetime`, every other unit keeps dates), the index stores them so, and a label writes a gap `2026.09.15 06:00–11:00 UTC`.
4. **The months of a series are joined into one series, and every month is read again when any changes.** Options: A) each month a series of its own (a dataset would have a row and a chip per month, a station's coverage would stop at every month's end); B) keep each month's rows and re-read only the month whose version changed (the gaps across a month's boundary depend on its neighbours, and the index would keep a second kind of series row); C) read every month's `series.json` when one changes and join them (`join_months`). The listings are small and never the Parquet files. Decision: C. The joined series' known periods are the union of the months'; a station's are its own periods in each month that has it (else that month's), and the periods of each month without it whose stations have no periods of their own: a country-wide source publishes every station there, so the station is known there without a value, while a month of a per-station source that lacks it leaves it unknown (as ADR-021 erratum 3 says of ČHMÚ). A variable's first and last are the earliest and latest of its months, its gaps every known unit between them without a value, across the months' boundaries too, and its counts the sums. The series' version is its latest month's, a station's file that of the latest month that has it, and `series_catalogs.months` records every month read (`[[month, slot, version], …]`), which the API gives as `months`. A month Obserwator refuses is left out with `series_unreadable`; the dataset has no coverage only when no month is left.
5. **The index is completed in place within schema version 1.** Options: A) raise `SCHEMA_VERSION` to 2, so the next start rebuilds the index; B) a migration framework, as the state database has; C) additions within version 1 that the first start makes in place. A empties the index, the job history included, makes the first start wait for a full scan (about 1,645 s for the test server's `agent-tmp`), and raising a format number needs the author's consent (REL-1.4); B builds machinery for a cache that is otherwise rebuilt. Decision: C, ECO-085's rule applied to the index: `initialize` adds what an index of version 1 lacks (`_ADDITIONS`) in one `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` transaction, looking for it again inside the transaction so two processes add it once, and answers `completed`, then `current`. A column is added last with `ALTER TABLE`, so a new and a completed index have the same columns; a table is created; a table whose constraint widens (`series_catalogs`, for its `time_unit` check and its new column `months`) is renamed, created again from its statement and given the old rows, then the old one is dropped. The additions of 0.4.0: `versions.tier`, `versions.publication`, `stations.groundwater_kind`, the table `skipped_series` and the rebuilt `series_catalogs`. A change an older reader cannot skip still raises the number and rebuilds.
6. **A series of a unit Archiwista does not know is skipped, with a record and one warning.** Options: A) the 0.3.3 behaviour, a producer error that leaves the dataset without coverage and warns `series_unreadable` on every scan; B) skip it silently; C) skip it, record its slots, warn once. ECO-085 asks a consumer to treat an unknown value as a skipped entry with a warning record, not a failure, and a warning repeated by every scheduled scan is noise. Decision: C. The adapter refuses such a series with `UnsupportedError` (reason `series_unit`, details `dataset` and `unit`), told from the registry by the dataset whose directory `series/versions/<slug>/` the version lies in, before the file is read. The scan keeps the dataset's versions listed, reads no coverage, records its slots and the refused versions in `skipped_series` and gives one notice `series_skipped` per dataset, again only when the version of one of its slots differs from the one recorded, and on a full scan. Obserwator's statistics (`time_unit` `statistics`) never form a series slot: they are archive versions.
7. **No chart of hours or weeks.** Options: A) draw them (hours need a chart over months of Parquet files with its own simplification; weekly readings fall on irregular dates); B) offer no chart and keep the coverage. Obserwator's interface draws the hourly chart (its ADR-031 D7, the live tail its step O6), and Archiwista's chart serves the daily, monthly and annual series. Decision: B. `chart_choices` leaves a series of a unit outside `CHART_ORDER` out of the card's `charts`; `GET /stations/{code}/series` without `dataset` never chooses one, and with `dataset` of one, like `GET /stations/compare` of one, answers `400` with reason `no_chart` (`details.unit`, the texts `api.chart_hour` and `api.chart_week`). The web card draws the daily series when the address names an hourly or weekly one.
8. **Private versions are shown with their tier and publication, and downloads do not change.** Options: A) hide `private` versions; B) refuse their download; C) show every version with its tier and publication state and leave the downloads as they are. Obserwator's ADR-030 D5 makes a private source readable by Archiwista's signed-in users, and Archiwista shows no data without a session; ECO-084's „honours `private` in its downloads in a later minor” concerns a public view without sign-in, which Archiwista does not have. Decision: C (the coordinator's ruling). `EntryMeta`, the index's `versions` and the API's `Version` carry `tier` and `publication` (`null`: none recorded, the verified archive and public); the CLI's version lines and the interface's badges say „telemetria: dane operacyjne, niezweryfikowane” or „dane operacyjne, niezweryfikowane” (Nogat DEV-20.8 as amended by ECO-084) and „publiczne” or „prywatne”.
9. **Groundwater stations are a third family that takes meteo's remembered year type.** Options: A) a third key `groundwater` in `year_types` of the preferences; B) the year type remembered for meteo data. A changes the preferences document and the switch for a family whose data, like meteo's, are numbered by calendar years. Decision: B (the coordinator's ruling): `YearTypes.of_kind` gives meteo's type to every family but hydro, and the interface's hint names „danych meteorologicznych i wód podziemnych”. The kind `groundwater` and `groundwater_kind` are new values of the station answers and of `?kind=`; the map draws the station as a diamond (Obserwator's symbol), brown where the type is shown without coverage.

**Consequences:** Archiwista 0.4.0 needs Obserwator 3.2 (the tag `v3.2.0`, `SUPPORTED_VERSIONS` `>=3.2.0,<4`) and is deployed on a shared store before Obserwator 3.2.0 writes captures or hourly series there; a store Obserwator 3.2.0 wrote to first gets its versions' tiers from one `archiwista reindex --store <store>`. The first start keeps the whole index, the job history included. The API gains `Version.tier` and `.publication`, `groundwater_kind`, `months`, `week` and `hour` as `time_unit`, UTC times in an hourly series' coverage and the refusal `no_chart`; the CLI prints the tier, the publication and `groundwater (bore)`. An hourly series' label is „nieznane” while its months do not fill a year (SPEC 4.8.4: a year is known when all its units are). A later unit of Obserwator costs Archiwista nothing but a warning until it chooses to read it. The spool of captures lies under the `state_dir` Obserwator sees in Archiwista's processes, so Archiwista runs with the `state_dir` of Obserwator's timers (USAGE, "Updating to 0.4.0"). SPEC 4.3, 4.4, 4.8.4, 4.8.7, 6.7, 9.2, 12.8 and 13, ARCHITECTURE, AGENT_API, USAGE and the CHANGELOG describe it.
