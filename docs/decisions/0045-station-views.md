# ADR-045: The station views: the routes they read and how the interface draws them

**Date:** 2026-10-26
**Status:** proposed (plan `docs/plans/2026-10-25-archiwista-next.md` Part 4, written with the defaults of D12 while its approval is open; done on the topic branch `feature/kartograf-data-output`; accepted when the author approves the plan and merges the branch)

**Context:** I6 (ADR 0032 decision 13) gave the stations of the station catalogs and the coverage of their series to agents and scripts: `GET /stations`, the card, the Timeline of one station and `GET /map/stations`. The interface had no view of them. The author answered the mockup questions M5 and M11 on 2026-10-26 (SPEC 4.8.4, 6.5 i to k and m, 6.7, 6.8): a point's shape says the station type and its fill the coverage of one variable, clusters are neutral, the Timeline is a tab of the Catalog, the comparison stacks one panel per station. Part 4 builds the read side of the station views of SPEC 13 "v0.5" on those routes, step by step (I8.1 to I8.6); each step adds a decision here.

**Options (for the whole part):**
- A) The interface reads only what the routes give, and each step adds the route it lacks (the datasets, the Timeline of a page, the series values, the comparison). Chosen: the CLI and agents get the same answers (SPEC 9), and the index stays the only source of labels.
- B) The interface derives what it needs from `GET /products` and the card of each station (the variables from the first station's series, the Timeline by one request per station). Rejected: a dataset's variables are not those of one station, and a page of 200 stations would be 200 requests.
- C) A chart library for the station chart. Rejected (D12): the interface has no build step (A-ADR-028) and the vendored libraries have no chart; an SVG drawn by the module is enough for two axes and the gap bands.

**Decision:** A, in the steps of Part 4.

1. **The datasets and the station type (I8.1, commit 1).** `GET /stations/datasets` (and `archiwista stations datasets`) lists the datasets with station series in the index, one per store and dataset of `series_catalogs`, by store and key: the dataset key, the producer's name of the segment of its series slot, provider, family, time unit, the default year type of its labels and R by default in it (`default_span`, as the labels count it), the label of the current series version with its `series.json`, and its variables (key, variable, unit, half-year period): the distinct keys of `station_series` in the order of the series index (`IndexQueries.station_datasets`). The interface's dataset chip and "Kolor punktów" read it, never a key's text (SPEC 4.2.1). `GET /stations` and `GET /map/stations` gain the repeatable `meteo_kind` (`synoptic`, `climate`, `precipitation`; `MeteoKind`, `METEO_KINDS`): the type chips filter on the server, so the counts and the grid count what the chips show; a station of no such kind (a water gauge, a ČHMÚ meteo station without a kind) is left out while the filter is given. The fixed paths are declared before `/stations/{code}`.
2. **The layer (I8.1, commit 2).** A product whose segments belong to the producer `imgw` (`segment.producer` of `GET /products`) is drawn as its stations (`map/stations.js`) in place of the outlines and the year layers: a GeoJSON source clustered by MapLibre (radius 42 px, up to zoom 13), a symbol layer whose `icon-image` is `<shape>-<state>`, one of 15 images drawn once on a canvas (a triangle for `hydro`, a circle for `precipitation`, a square for a climatological, synoptic or untyped meteo station; green solid, orange solid, orange lower half, grey hollow, grey dashed outline), the name and, for a water gauge, the river from zoom 8, and neutral clusters with their count. A click on a cluster eases to `getClusterExpansionZoom`; a cluster of up to 8 stations at one position (or one that cannot expand below `maxZoom`) spreads them on a 34 px circle with leader lines until the next click, a zoom or `Esc`. Above `limit` (5,000) the answer's cells are the same neutral circles at their centres, sized by their count, and a click fits the cell. The tooltip gives the name, type, river, "na granicy obszaru" and the label of every variable of the point's `coverage` (P16), the "Kolor punktów" one marked. The choices live in the address apart from the filters of files, so `/map/footprints` never gets them: `dataset`, `color`, `meteo_kind` (a comma list) and `year_type`, cleared by another product (another dataset clears `color`), and `boundary=false`. The panel has the dataset chips (`GET /stations/datasets` of the product's family, the first by default), the type chips of a meteo or mixed product and "Uwzględnij stacje na granicy obszaru" in place of the variants and the filters of files; the bar has "Kolor punktów" (the dataset's variables, the first by default) and the counters "N w widoku" and "N bez położenia" (`counts.unlocated`), which opens the Catalog with `location=none`, the product, the dataset and the types (M5). The legend names the three shapes, the five fills (drawn in the shape of the product's kind) and the cluster. `GET /map/stations` gets `bbox`, `area_id`, `boundary`, `dataset`, `kind` (the product when it is a station kind), `meteo_kind`, `year_type`, `years`, `limit=5000` and `grid_columns`. The map waits for the products before it asks for outlines or stations, and for the datasets before the stations. A browser that cannot draw the map (no WebGL2, as headless Firefox) still reports the view the address, or else the home view, frames, so its counters and their links work.
3. **The remembered year type (I8.2, commit 1; P15).** `AppSettings.year_types` (`YearTypes`: `hydro` hydrological and `meteo` calendar by default, the numbering of the archives) is kept in `config.toml` as a view preference (SPEC 4.3), answered by `GET /settings` and changed by `PUT /settings/year-types`, which replaces only these two (an omitted one takes its default) with the permission of `PUT /settings` (`settings.write`) until accounts exist (v0.4, when `/preferences` ties it to a person). The station routes and `archiwista stations` number a view without `year_type` by the type remembered for each dataset's family (`label_year_type` with `year_types`, in place of the fixed family default of ADR 0032 decision 12); a view of both families (`GET /stations` without `dataset`, `kind` or `meteo_kind`) takes the type remembered for hydro data (`StationFilters.view_year_type`, SPEC 6.7); a given `year_type` wins and changes nothing; half-year characteristics stay hydrological. `GET /stations/datasets` gives each dataset the type of its family and R in it. Options: a cookie or the browser's storage (rejected: the CLI and agents would not see it, and SPEC 4.3 keeps view preferences in the configuration), `/preferences` now (rejected: a preference of a person needs accounts, v0.4).
4. **Search and order (I8.2, commit 2).** `GET /stations` and `archiwista stations list` take `q`: the stations whose name or river contains the text, both case-folded as Python folds them (`str.casefold`, the SQL function `casefold` that `index.schema.connect` registers, since SQLite's `lower` folds ASCII letters only and `łódź` would miss `Łódź`), or whose code begins with it; `instr` and `substr` read every character literally, so `_`, `%` or `-` are no wildcards (as ADR 0032 decision 11 keeps `LIKE` away from codes); a blank `q` searches nothing. And `sort`: `river`, the default, the order along rivers of SPEC 6.8 (the hydrographic code as text, so a tributary, whose code begins with its river's, follows the river; then `river_order`, the station's place on the provider's list; then the name; a station without a code or an order after those with one), or `name`; then code, provider and store, which make every key unique. The keys of the order are selected with the page and kept in its cursor, and `q` and `sort` take part in the cursor's fingerprint. `GET /map/stations` keeps the name order. Options: an FTS5 table of names (rejected: a page of a search with its counts over 6,000 synthetic stations takes about 20 ms on the development machine, a part inside a word is no token prefix, and a second index of names would have to follow every new catalog); `LIKE` with escaped wildcards (rejected: its case folding is ASCII only, and decision 11 of ADR 0032 keeps it away from codes); the order by `river_km` (rejected: not every catalog gives it, and it is not the provider's list).
5. **The Catalog list of stations (I8.2, commit 3).** The Catalog lists the stations of a product whose segments belong to the producer `imgw` (as the map does, decision 2) in place of its files (`views/catalog-stations.js`): `GET /stations` page by page (200 rows, the keyset cursor) with the parameters of the address, the station kind being the product's (`hydro`, `meteo`; the product `stations` holds both and has a kind field). The located stations come first under their heading with its count ("Z położeniem", "W obszarze" with an area), then "Bez położenia", read with `location=none` once the located pages are all read, so each group pages by its own cursor and `counts` gives both headings; that group is folded when more than 50 stations are located and unfolded when the address asks for `location=none`, as the map's counter does (M5); its rows say the catalog's reason (no coordinates in the sources, 0,0, outside the provider's country) and never lead to the map. A row has the columns of sketch 6.5 m (station and type, code and provider, river and its hydrographic code, coverage, location) and a line per variable of each series: its symbol with the fill of its state (`StationSymbol`, the shapes and fills of the map), key, label and full years (P16: no single label of a station). `views/catalog-grid.js` takes a kind of row (`GridRows`: each row's height, cells and header; the heights differ, so the rows' tops are summed once and the visible ones found by binary search), and files and stations share one virtualised grid and its keys (arrows, Page Up/Down, Home/End; Enter or a click shows a located station on the map at zoom 11 and folds or unfolds "Bez położenia" on its heading; M shows a station on the map). The order switch writes `sort` (along rivers by default, or the name) and the search field `q`; the filter panel has the fields of `GET /stations` (dataset, kind or meteo types, river code with "z dopływami", active, variable, the coverage chips of each variable of the dataset with its least full years, the range of the labels, the area and its boundary) and the switch "Lata: hydrologiczne / kalendarzowe" (M11 point 5): a choice is `year_type` in the address, so a link and a reload keep it, and for a session with `settings.write` also the type remembered for the list's family (`PUT /settings/year-types`, decision 3); without `year_type` the switch shows the remembered one. `counts.hidden` is said above the rows ("Ukryte przez filtry pokrycia: N", P14). Until the station card (I8.5) a row leads to the map only.
6. **The years of a page (I8.3, commit 1).** `GET /stations/timeline` (and `archiwista stations timeline`) pages the stations of one dataset with the filters, order and cursor of `GET /stations` (`StationTimelineListFilters`: `StationListFilters` with `dataset` required): each item is the station (store, provider, code, name, kind and meteo kind, river with its hydrographic code, active, location) with its series year by year, the `TimelineSeries` that `GET /stations/{code}/coverage` gives for the same `dataset`, `variable`, `years` and `year_type` (`TimelineSeries.of`, shared by both routes), so the Timeline tab needs one request a page. `variable` (repeatable) keeps both of its meanings: as in `GET /stations` the stations with a value of each variable, and as in the coverage route only their rows; the tab thus keeps the list's filters, and it never sends a variable the person did not choose, as that would hide stations (P14). No index query is new: `IndexQueries.stations` labels the series of a page together (`_stations_coverage`, one read of `series_years` per year type), and `station_list` and `station_timeline_page` share the reading of a page (`_station_page`). The page the tab asks for, 200 water gauges with H, Q and T over 75 years (45,000 years, 3.3 MB of JSON), first took about 415 ms (median) on the development machine; it now takes about 210 ms (p95 263–271 ms; SPEC 4.5 allows 300 ms): the years are read as plain tuples (`row_factory = None`; `SeriesYear` is a named tuple), grouped per series and taken whole when the station's R is the one read; a label goes over a variable's years once and dates only its longest gap; a year of the Timeline is a record (`TimelineYear`, a `TypedDict`: the same JSON and schema) written as a dict literal; a Timeline row (`TimelineVariable`) is built with `model_construct`, its fields coming typed from the index. For a person the command prints under each station a line per variable with a character per year (`█` full, `▒` with gaps, `·` without a value, `?` not known) and its label. Options: a request per station (rejected: option B); a `variable` that only selects rows (rejected: the tab would lose the list's variable filter); a page with the "Kolor punktów" variable only and the other rows asked when a station expands (not now: a parameter the plan does not name, and the three variables of a daily dataset fit the budget).
7. **The Timeline tab (I8.3, commit 2).** "Oś czasu" stands beside "Lista" in the toolbar of a station product's Catalog (`layout=timeline`; the list stays the default, M3) and keeps every filter of the address: the tab reads `GET /stations/timeline` with the list's parameters in one cursor of the address's location group (`location`, every station by default, along rivers: the Timeline has no group headings, the group chips still filter), of one dataset, the address's or else the product's first, which the filter panel then shows in place of "Wszystkie zbiory" (a coverage filter chosen there writes that dataset to the address, since it needs one). A page asks for 600 variable rows: 200 stations of a daily hydro dataset, 22 of half-year characteristics with 27 keys, never fewer than 20. `views/catalog-station-timeline.js` draws an ARIA treegrid: a station row (level 1) with the years of the "Kolor punktów" variable of the address (`color`, shared with the map, the dataset's first by default; the chips "Wiersz stacji"), chosen on the client among the variables of the answer, which expands into a row per variable (level 2). A year is a cell of the API's kind: `full` the green of "pełne" (SPEC 6.4), `fraction` the same green lighter in proportion to the share of the known units with a value (opacity 0.16 to 0.86), `none` a grey hollow box, `unknown` a grey dashed outline; a year outside the series' R stays blank. The legend names the four kinds; each column header names its year by the convention ("r.h. 2024 (2023.11.01–2024.10.31)", "2024 (kalendarzowy)") and shows its number every 10 years (every 5 up to 40 columns, each up to 12); one tooltip (`role=tooltip`, also of the focused cell) gives the year, its kind, its units, known units and units with a value, the variable's label and its longest gap. Rows have one height (26 px) and are drawn only near the view; one cell is the tab stop: arrows move by year and row, Page Up/Down by a view, Home/End to a row's ends (with Ctrl the grid's), Enter or Space folds a station. Options: a canvas or one SVG strip per row (rejected: a year would be no element for the keys, the tooltip and screen readers); the "Kolor punktów" variable sent as `variable` (rejected, decision 6: it hides the stations without it); the list's two location groups (rejected: one cursor keeps the order along rivers of every station, P14).
8. **The values of a station's series (I8.4, commit 1).** Nothing read a series file before: the labels come from the index (ADR 0032 decision 12), and a chart needs the values. The optional capability `SeriesValuesAdapter` reads them. `read_values(root, version, *, station, variables, start, end)` yields a `SeriesValues` per unit of the station's file: its first day, the half-year period of a characteristic, the value of each variable or `None` where the producer flags the unit missing, the variables holding an accumulated sum, and the first and last time of each dated half-year extreme. The IMGW adapter maps `imgwtools.read_series`, which reads the Parquet file and checks it against the series index (Archiwista imports no `pyarrow`); a station outside the series is `NotFoundError`, any other refusal the producer's `ProducerError`. `characteristics_dataset(dataset)` names the dataset of the half-year characteristics: the one other dataset of the same provider and family whose unit is the half-year in the producer's registry (`imgwtools.datasets()`: `provider`, `kind`, `time_unit`; `pl.imgw.hydro_semiannual` for daily and monthly hydro data of IMGW-PIB, none for meteo or ČHMÚ data), never from the key's text (SPEC 4.2.1). `extremes_of(dataset, variable)` names the characteristics that hold a variable's least and greatest value of each half-year period (`{"min": "Q_min", "max": "Q_max"}`): IMGWTools names a characteristic after its variable and statistic, and the adapter takes one only when the characteristics dataset holds it in the variable's unit. Options: the names of the extremes formed in the chart's service (rejected: the producer's naming would leave the adapter, A-ADR-014); a missing flag beside a `None` value (rejected: the producer gives `None` exactly for `missing`).
9. **The chart of a series (I8.4, commit 2).** `GET /stations/{code}/series` (and `archiwista stations series`) gives one or two variables of a station's series as a chart, for display only (SPEC 5.7, 6.7): `station_chart` of `api/stations.py` finds the station and its series in the index (`dataset`, or else the one series of the station with every variable; another variable is `400 unknown_variable`, several series `400 ambiguous_dataset`, half-year characteristics `400 half_year_chart`), and `services/series_chart.py` reads the current version's values through `SeriesValuesAdapter.read_values`. The range (`from`, `to`; by default the series' first and last unit) is counted in calendar units, so a unit the station's file lacks has no value either. Up to `max_points` units (≤ 2,000) a point is a unit's value; a longer range is cut from its first unit into intervals of `ceil(units / max_points)` units (`interval`), each with the least, mean and greatest value of its units, `null` without one, never interpolated. `gaps` are the producer's gaps of the variable from the index (`CoverageVariable.gaps`) cut at the range's ends, so a gap shorter than an interval keeps its band and a unit without a value outside them stays not known. An accumulated sum is a marker and no value of its day; the extremes of the dataset's half-year characteristics (`characteristics_dataset`, `extremes_of`), when the index holds that series of the station, are markers at the day IMGW published (`min`, `max`, with the period and the last occurrence), read once. The answer (`StationChart`) names the series version, the station's file with its SHA-256, the licence and the formula of the version. `version`, when given, must be the index's current one (`409 series_version_not_current`): the index keeps the gaps of the current version only. Options: gaps derived from the values (rejected: a missing value is a gap or a unit not known alike, decision 8); an older version read with its own `series.json` (not now: no fixture holds two series versions, and the card shows the current one); a line of half-year characteristics (not now: three overlapping periods a year; their extremes are the markers of the daily chart).
10. **The station card (I8.5, commit 1).** `station=<store>/<provider>/<code>` (`STATION_PARAM`, `stationAddress` of `map/stations.js`; codes of both providers hold no `/`) opens `views/station.js` over the current view (an overlay of `routes.js`, as the file card), over `GET /stations/{code}` with `store`, `provider` and the address's `year_type`. A click on a station's point (its tooltip says so), a row of the Catalog's list (Enter or a click; `M` and the row's button still show it on the map) and a station's name on the Timeline (or `K` on its rows; Enter and Space still fold) open it. The header names the type, the river with its hydrographic code, the code, provider and state, with the Catalog's year-type switch (`views/year-type.js` `useYearType`, shared with the filter panel: the address's `year_type`, else the type remembered for the station's family; an administrator's choice is remembered), then the location with its source, the catalog's date and uncertainty, or the catalog's reason, and "Strona stacji IMGW" for a water gauge of IMGW-PIB (`https://hydro.imgw.pl/#/station/hydro/<code>`, SPEC 6.7: a plain link, no request; SPEC names no page of other stations, so they have none). The actions are "Pokaż na mapie" with a position (the map of the station's product at zoom 11; the card closes) and "Kopiuj ścieżkę pliku"; then the coverage of each variable of each series (label with its fill, full years, longest gap; P16), the tabs "Metadane", "Pochodzenie" (each series version: label, the station's file here and as aliased, SHA-256, `series.json`, licence; the catalog version and the source of the position) and "Szczegóły techniczne" (the API's answer) in `station_tab`, and the licence with each distinct formula and "Kopiuj atrybucję". `GET /stations/{code}` gains `paths`, each series file by dataset on this computer and as mounted on others (`CopyPath` of `copy_path`, as the file card's), since a path relative to the store is no path to copy. The chart ("Wykres") is commit 2 and "Porównaj z…" comes with I8.6. Options: the card as a view of its own (rejected: SPEC 6.2 opens it over the current view, like the file card); the absolute path built by the interface from `GET /settings` (rejected: the roots and aliases are the server's, and not every session reads the settings).
11. **The chart (I8.5, commit 2).** `views/station-chart.js` draws the tab "Wykres", now the card's default (`station_tab` without a value), as SVG (no library, option C): one series of the station (`chart_dataset`, else the address's `dataset`, else its first series not of half-year characteristics, which are markers, never a line) and one or two of its variables (`chart_variable`, `H` or `H,Q`; by default the address's `color`, else the series' first; "Druga zmienna" adds one, on the right axis when its unit differs), read from `GET /stations/{code}/series` with `max_points` the plot's width in pixels (at most 2,000). Without `from` and `to` the range is the API's default, the series' first to last unit, the whole available period (SPEC 6.7): simplified when it has more units than the plot has pixels (a band of the least and greatest values under the mean line, the note "wykres uproszczony: min./śr./maks. z N dni (cały okres; po przybliżeniu wartości dobowe)"), each day's value otherwise. A drag selection (every day it touches; a history entry), the wheel (around the pointer) and the keys `+`, `-` (around the middle) and the arrows (a fifth of the range; the wheel and the keys replace the entry) write `from` and `to`, at least 3 days and kept within the series (the API's default range, else the first to the last value of the variables); a range that holds the whole series, `Home` and "Cały okres" remove them. A point lies in the middle of its days; the line breaks at a point without a value and at a gap between two points (a new subpath; a lone point is `M x,y h0` with a dot); each of `gaps` is a grey band over its days (with two variables a strip of the variable's colour above it); a unit without a value outside them has no band (not known, decision 9). Extremes are dots and an accumulated sum a diamond, each with its text as a tooltip; the pointer's point gives its days and each value, "luka" or "nieznana". A line sums the range, each variable's points with a value and its gaps. The card's own parameters (`station_tab`, `from`, `to`, `chart_dataset`, `chart_variable`) go when it closes. `ChartPlot` and `TimeAxis` are exported for the comparison.

**Consequences:**
- The variables of a dataset are those its stations' series hold (the same set `imgwtools.summarize_series` gives); their order is that of the series index, which IMGWTools writes by key, not the registry's (P before Tmax for ČHMÚ meteo data, H, Q, T for hydro data). The first one is the default colour of the points (D12).
- A dataset whose series version is known but whose stations have no series lists no variables; the map then has no colour to choose.
- `meteo_kind` takes part in the cursor of `GET /stations`: a cursor of another selection of types is refused like any other.
- `q` and `sort` take part in the cursor of `GET /stations` too, so a page of another search or order is refused. A search scans the stations of the other filters (no index serves a part of a name); the code prefix is compared with `substr`, not the range over the index of codes, because the name and the river need the scan anyway.
- The product `stations` (the catalogs themselves) has no dataset: every located station, in the grey dashed outline of an unknown coverage, and the legend says so.
- A click on a station point opens nothing in I8.1: the card comes with I8.5. The year-type switch stands in the Catalog (I8.2) and the card; the map follows `year_type` of the address.
- The remembered year types take part in the cursor of `GET /stations`: a page asked after the choice changed is refused like a cursor of another query, so a list never mixes two numberings.
- Until accounts exist one choice serves everyone, and only a session with `settings.write` (the local owner, an administrator) changes it; the interface still switches `year_type` in the address for anyone.
- The canvas is not asserted pixel by pixel: the shapes and fills are checked in the screenshot `docs/reports/v0.5-screenshots/01-stacje-na-mapie.webp`; `tests/e2e/test_stations_map.py` asserts the tooltip, the cluster, the spread, the grid, the legend, the counter and the chips.
- The Catalog's list numbers a list of both families (the product `stations` without a dataset, kind or type) by hydro's remembered type and its switch remembers hydro's, as the API numbers it (decision 3).
- The two location groups are two queries, each with its own cursor: "Bez położenia" is not asked for while it is folded or before the located stations are all read, and a folded group shows only its count.
- `tests/e2e/test_stations_catalog.py` (Chromium and Firefox) compares the rows with `GET /stations` in both orders, the reasons, the counter of the map, the fold above 50 located stations (the counts rewritten with `page.route`), the river and coverage filters with the hidden count, the year-type switch with a reload and the remembered type, and the keys; the screenshot `docs/reports/v0.5-screenshots/02-katalog-stacji.webp` shows the list.
- A page of the Timeline costs in proportion to its stations, variables and years: a station of half-year characteristics has up to 27 keys, nine times the rows of a daily water gauge, so the tab asks such a dataset for fewer stations a page.
- `GET /stations/{code}/coverage` shares `TimelineSeries`, `TimelineVariable` and `TimelineYear`, its answer unchanged; `GET /stations` of 200 stations with three variables went from about 255 to 200 ms (median) with the faster reading of the years.
- `tests/integration/test_station_timeline_api.py` compares each item with the coverage route over every dataset of `complete_pl_cz` (also with `variable`, a range in calendar years and the name order), joins pages of two stations, refuses a missing `dataset` as the CLI does, compares the route with the CLI page after page and the CLI's strips with the route's cells, and measures the page of 200 stations (`-m perf`, a synthetic index written with `replace_stations` and `replace_series`).
- A station of the Timeline is a station with a series of its dataset (decision 6); one whose series lacks the "Kolor punktów" variable says "brak zmiennej …" in its row and leaves its years blank, and its own variables still expand.
- The size of a Timeline page belongs to its query, so a cursor is never asked with another size; the year type comes from the answer, so the headers and the cells change together when the switch "Lata" changes `year_type`.
- `tests/e2e/test_stations_timeline.py` (Chromium and Firefox) opens the tab from the list with its filters and back, compares the station row of `149190310` with `GET /stations/{code}/coverage` of H and of Q (the chip "Wiersz stacji"), reads the longest gap, the units and the label in the tooltip of a `fraction` cell, expands and folds a station by keys with the focus moving by year and row, and checks the headers and cells in calendar years; the screenshot `docs/reports/v0.5-screenshots/03-os-czasu-stacji.webp` shows the four kinds of cell and the tooltip (ČHMÚ data of `complete_pl_cz` in calendar years).
- A value of `None` does not say whether its unit is a gap or not known: the chart takes its bands from the gap lists of the index. In `complete` the January of `149190310` has no value and no gap (no archive holds it), so H has values on 2023.12.01 and 2024.02.29 only.
- No fixture store holds half-year characteristics yet (Requests of the plan), so `extremes_of` is pinned against the producer's registry (`tests/contract/test_imgw_values.py`) and the extremes of a chart by its unit test (I8.4, commit 2).
- A chart costs the read of one station's file by the producer: building the chart of 27,393 days took 15 ms (median, p95 16 ms; SPEC 4.5 allows 500 ms with the read), measured by the `perf` test of `tests/integration/test_station_series_api.py`; the read of a real 75-year file is measured on the author's store. In `complete` H of `149190310` gives values on 2023.12.01 and 2024.02.29 only, with two bands, and January without a band.
